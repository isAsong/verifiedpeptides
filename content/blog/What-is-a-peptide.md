# Molecular Biology Fundamentals of Peptides: From Structural Definitions to Physiological Regulation

In molecular and cell biology, **peptides** serve as the essential molecular bridge connecting fundamental nutrients (amino acids) to complex functional macromolecules (proteins). They are not merely the incomplete hydrolysis products of proteins but represent a distinct class of naturally occurring bioactive substances widely distributed across biological systems. In-depth research into peptides has opened a critical window for understanding the regulatory mechanisms governing life activities.

## Chemical Nature and the Peptide Bond: The Basis of Covalent Linkage

From a rigorous chemical perspective, a **peptide** is a polymer formed by the covalent linkage of two or more amino acids through **peptide bonds**. A peptide bond is specifically an amide bond formed by the condensation reaction between the α-carboxyl group of one amino acid and the α-amino group of another, with the elimination of one water molecule.

Crucially, once amino acids are linked via peptide bonds, their original free amino and carboxyl groups are no longer in their free forms, as they have participated in bond formation. Therefore, the building blocks within peptide and protein chains are referred to as **amino acid residues**. In nature, physiologically active peptides are predominantly composed of **L-type α-amino acids**.

## Oligopeptides, Polypeptides, and Proteins: A Refined Classification System

In academia, peptides are categorized based on both the number of amino acid residues in the chain and their corresponding molecular weight. This classification reflects not only nomenclature differences but also their distinct physicochemical properties and biological activities:

**1. Classification by the Number of Amino Acids:**
- **Dipeptide**: Contains 2 amino acids.
- **Tripeptide**: Contains 3 amino acids.
- **Tetrapeptide**: Contains 4 amino acids, and so forth.
- **Oligopeptide**: Generally refers to chains containing fewer than 10 amino acids.
- **Polypeptide**: Refers to chains containing 10 or more amino acids.
- **Protein**: When the polypeptide chain is sufficiently large, typically exceeding 50 amino acids, it is classified as a protein.

**2. Classification by Molecular Weight Range (Daltons):**
- The molecular weight range for bioactive "peptides" is generally defined between **5000 and 180 Da**.
- **Large Peptides**: Molecular weight range between 5000 and 3000 Da.
- **Polypeptides**: Molecular weight range between 3000 and 1000 Da.
- **Small Peptides / Oligopeptides / Low-Molecular-Weight Peptides**: Molecular weight range between 1000 and 180 Da, typically composed of 2 to 6 amino acids. Peptides in this range exhibit exceptionally high bioavailability and potent physiological regulatory efficacy due to their minimal molecular size.

## Biological Functions: Key Players in Cellular Signal Transduction

Peptides are among the most fundamental substance bases of life, with their influence extending to virtually every aspect of physiological processes. Over 100 distinct active peptides have been identified as critical **neurotransmitters**, **neuromodulators**, and **hormones** within both the central and peripheral nervous systems.

At the cellular level, peptides participate in intercellular communication via specific receptor-mediated **signal transduction** pathways. They profoundly modulate the homeostatic regulation of the cardiovascular, immune, and digestive systems. At the molecular level, peptides are deeply involved in metabolic regulation, tissue repair and regeneration, and the mediation of inflammatory and nociceptive (pain) responses.

## Technological Advancements and Application Prospects

With the continuous maturation of solid-phase peptide synthesis, recombinant gene technology, and advanced purification techniques, peptide products—once prohibitively expensive and confined to fundamental frontier research—are now widely integrated into everyday applications. In the functional cosmetics industry, for instance, peptides have emerged as premier active ingredients for anti-wrinkle and anti-aging formulations. Furthermore, specific bioactive peptides have demonstrated unparalleled potential in reducing under-eye bags and dark circles, skin brightening and body contouring, and promoting hair growth.
